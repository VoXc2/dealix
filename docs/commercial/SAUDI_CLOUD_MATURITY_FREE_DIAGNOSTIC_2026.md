## Current official Saudi signal

On 2026-09-03, the Communications, Space and Technology Commission (CST) launched the **Cloud Market Maturity Index** to support competition, service quality, innovation and customer satisfaction in the Saudi cloud market.

CST states that the index targets cloud service providers registered with the Commission and evaluates service maturity across **six main dimensions** and **33 sub-indicators** spanning technical, operational and commercial dimensions:

1. service diversity and comprehensiveness;
2. technical expertise and technical skills;
3. customer satisfaction;
4. strategic partnerships;
5. service-delivery efficiency;
6. sales and marketing.

Official source: https://www.cst.gov.sa/media-center/news/N2026090303

CST announced the top-ranked providers at the index checkpoint as Google Cloud, Oracle, STC Solutions, Huawei Cloud and STC, with Edarat Group recognized in the SME category.

These rankings are **market evidence only**. They do not prove that any provider is the best choice for a specific customer, that Dealix has a commercial relationship with a listed provider, or that a customer should migrate, buy, renew or terminate any service.

## Dealix commercial use

Use the CST dimensions as evidence-grounded lenses for a **free cloud operating-readiness diagnostic** when a customer, partner or internal Dealix sector pod has a bounded cloud problem such as:

- cloud cost, ownership or service boundaries are unclear;
- a cloud migration/cutover exists without explicit acceptance or rollback evidence;
- workloads use multiple providers without a decision/evidence layer;
- AI/agent workloads need a governed cloud/data/model boundary;
- backups exist but restore evidence is weak;
- service health, SLOs, incidents or change evidence are fragmented;
- a customer is selecting or reviewing a cloud/MSP partner;
- cloud operations depend on manual approvals, tickets, spreadsheets or chat.

Public market evidence is never promoted directly to pipeline. A real reciprocal interaction or customer-authorized diagnostic is required before customer-specific qualification.

## Free diagnostic framework

### A. Service diversity and scope fit
- What business services and workloads are actually in scope?
- Which are IaaS, PaaS, SaaS, data, AI/ML, integration or managed-service dependencies?
- Is every material service tied to a named business owner and criticality?
- Which capabilities are genuinely needed versus merely available in a provider catalog?
- What dependency concentration or portability risk exists?

### B. Technical expertise and operating competence
- Who can own architecture, platform, security, data, FinOps and incident response?
- Which skills are internal, provider-owned or partner-dependent?
- Is operational knowledge documented and independently verifiable?
- Are privileged actions and break-glass procedures bounded?
- Can the team operate and recover the chosen platform without one irreplaceable individual?

### C. Customer / internal-user outcome evidence
- What measurable outcome is the cloud service expected to improve?
- What is the current baseline for availability, latency, deployment lead time, recovery, cost or support burden?
- What evidence exists for user/customer satisfaction instead of activity volume?
- Which complaints, incidents or recurring friction points are evidenced rather than assumed?
- What would constitute acceptance after a change?

### D. Strategic partner and supplier readiness
- Which provider/MSP/SI responsibilities are explicit?
- What is the exact legal entity and current service scope?
- Are support/escalation paths and subcontract boundaries known?
- Does the proposed partner add distinct value or duplicate another provider?
- Are data location, third-party processing, exit and portability boundaries documented?
- Public provider ranking/listing != Dealix partnership or customer fit.

### E. Service-delivery efficiency
- Is there a current service inventory and dependency map?
- Are incidents, requests, changes and problems traceable to owners and evidence?
- Are SLOs/SLAs and escalation thresholds explicit where relevant?
- Is observability sufficient to detect failure before customer impact becomes ambiguous?
- Are deploy/rollback identities exact and auditable?
- Are backup and restore procedures actually tested?
- Which repetitive operational steps can be safely automated first?

### F. Commercial / FinOps readiness
- Who owns cloud cost and budget authority?
- Which charges are fixed, variable, committed or usage-sensitive?
- Can cost be attributed to a customer, product, environment or workload?
- Are idle, duplicate or over-provisioned resources evidenced?
- Are committed-spend discounts or contractual terms understood before recommendation?
- What is the customer-specific economic case for change?
- Never infer savings percentages without the customer's actual bill and baseline.

## AI / agent cloud extension

When the cloud scope includes AI agents or model workloads, also classify:

- `DATA_SENSITIVITY = PUBLIC / INTERNAL / CONFIDENTIAL / RESTRICTED`;
- deterministic vs model-requiring steps;
- approved provider/model/data-processing boundary;
- cost authority and hard budget limit;
- secret isolation and tool/API permissions;
- human approval for consequential external actions;
- prompt/tool-injection and third-party risk;
- local/private fallback where appropriate;
- model/provider outage fallback;
- independent verification and immutable execution receipts.

A worker, environment variable, SDK default or provider credential must not become model, data, cost or action authority by itself.

## Evidence requested

Request only evidence appropriate to the authorized diagnostic. Do not ask customers to paste secrets into chat.

Minimum useful evidence may include:
- architecture/service inventory;
- billing/cost-export samples with sensitive data minimized;
- SLO/SLA and incident/change samples;
- monitoring/observability coverage;
- backup and restore evidence;
- role/responsibility and supplier boundaries;
- workload/data classification;
- deployment/rollback evidence;
- approved business outcome and current baseline.

## Readiness scoring

Score each CST-aligned dimension from evidence only:

- `0 = UNKNOWN / NO_USABLE_EVIDENCE`
- `1 = AD_HOC / MATERIAL_GAPS`
- `2 = DEFINED / PARTIAL_EVIDENCE`
- `3 = OPERATING / EVIDENCE_BACKED`

Never average away a blocker. Any unresolved production authority, restricted-data boundary, security, restore/rollback, provider responsibility, cost authority, customer acceptance or legal/regulatory dependency can keep the overall decision at HOLD.

## Diagnostic output

Produce:

1. current cloud/service map;
2. evidence vs assumptions ledger;
3. six-dimension CST-aligned readiness matrix;
4. top five operational/economic evidence gaps;
5. top three continuity/security/delivery risks;
6. provider/partner responsibility map;
7. FinOps baseline or explicit `COST_BASELINE_NOT_PROVEN`;
8. automation/AI candidates ranked by value, reversibility and evidence burden;
9. smallest safe test with acceptance and rollback;
10. qualified-discovery agenda;
11. customer-specific quote inputs only after discovery.

Decision state must be one of:
- `OPTIMIZE_CURRENT`
- `ADAPT_EXISTING`
- `QUALIFIED_PARTNER`
- `BOUNDED_EXPERIMENT`
- `HOLD_MISSING_EVIDENCE`
- `DECLINE_OR_DEFER`
