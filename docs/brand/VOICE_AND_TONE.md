# Dealix Voice and Tone

## Core voice

Dealix sounds like an accountable Saudi B2B operator: **calm, precise, evidence-led, commercially aware and technically credible**.

The voice is not consumer-AI excitement, management-consulting abstraction or agency hype.

## Voice principles

### 1. Evidence before adjectives
Prefer a source, mechanism, control or observed problem over words such as `powerful`, `revolutionary` or `advanced`.

### 2. State known vs unknown
Use explicit language for:
- verified fact;
- observed signal;
- hypothesis;
- estimate;
- draft;
- planned capability;
- verified outcome.

### 3. Explain the operating mechanism
Good Dealix copy shows how the system moves from reality to decision to controlled action to proof.

### 4. Calm confidence
Confident does not mean absolute. Avoid inflated certainty, fear tactics and urgency manufactured without evidence.

### 5. Saudi-native, not merely translated
Arabic copy should be authored for Saudi B2B readers. Preserve meaning and authority across Arabic and English rather than translating sentence structure literally.

### 6. Accountability stays visible
Do not imply that AI removes accountable owners, approval gates or customer responsibility when those still exist.

### 7. One next action
Commercial copy should normally lead to one useful next step, not a menu of unrelated CTAs.

## Preferred vocabulary

Use when accurate:
- governed
- evidence / proof
- operating context
- priority
- owner
- approval
- controlled execution
- baseline
- outcome
- source / provenance
- decision
- next action
- bounded Pilot
- stop / expand / redesign
- Saudi-first
- Arabic-first where appropriate

Avoid or heavily qualify:
- fully autonomous
- autopilot
- guaranteed
- 10x
- instant ROI
- zero-touch
- replaces your team
- revolutionary
- game-changing
- AI employee
- unlimited
- compliant / certified without scope and evidence

## Tone by surface

### Website
Clear category first, then mechanism, trust, evidence and CTA. Reduce feature-list density.

### Founder LinkedIn
Human operator POV. Share a real observation, a useful operating principle or a field lesson. Manual/native conversation. Avoid turning every post into a product advertisement.

### Dealix company channels
Product method, architecture, proof, governance, buyer education and permissioned customer evidence.

### Executive deck / proposal
Decision-defensibility tone: problem, scope, controls, evidence, economics, risk, acceptance, exit criteria.

### Diagnostic
Curious and precise. Do not prematurely sell. Separate observed evidence from hypotheses and ask for the minimum additional evidence needed.

### Event conversation
Short and problem-led. Ask useful questions before giving a platform monologue.

### Email / eligible WhatsApp
Contextual, source-bound and easy to understand. Never fake familiarity or a mutual connection. Respect channel/consent/suppression authority.

### Technical / trust material
Exact. Define boundaries, assumptions, known risks and what evidence exists. Do not turn security language into marketing theatre.

## Buying-group adaptation

The core story stays the same; emphasis changes by role.

- Founder/GM: priority, control, speed, visibility, bounded decision.
- Revenue: leakage, next action, follow-up, proposal, proof.
- Operations: ownership, workflow, acceptance, burden.
- Finance/procurement: scope, economics, reversibility, evidence.
- IT/security: access, integration, least privilege, auditability.
- Legal/governance: provenance, consent, approvals, accountability.

Never change factual claims or commercial authority merely to satisfy a persona.

## Arabic style

Prefer modern professional Saudi Arabic with necessary English product terms when they improve precision. Avoid excessive dialect, bureaucratic formalism and literal English syntax.

Examples of preferred patterns:
- `وش نعرف فعليًا؟` can be suitable in founder-native informal content.
- `ما الذي نعرفه فعليًا؟` is better for formal executive material.
- `الإثبات قبل التوسع` is preferred over grand claims about transformation.

Use the right register for the surface; do not force one Arabic register everywhere.

## Copy test

Before approval ask:
1. Is the first sentence useful to this buyer?
2. What claim requires evidence?
3. Is any estimate phrased as a fact?
4. Does the copy explain why Dealix rather than merely say it is AI?
5. Can a hidden buyer understand the risk/control boundary?
6. Is there one clear next action?
7. Would the copy still be credible if read by Legal, Finance and IT together?

## Anti-patterns

Reject copy that:
- says Dealix guarantees revenue or ROI;
- presents synthetic/demo evidence as customer proof;
- claims blanket PDPL compliance/certification without exact support;
- invents customer logos, testimonials, partnerships or government access;
- calls Dealix a generic AI automation agency;
- hides approval or data boundaries;
- uses urgency unsupported by a real deadline/signal;
- contains generic AI filler that could belong to any vendor.
